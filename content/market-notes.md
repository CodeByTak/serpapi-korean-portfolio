# Market plan notes (source for content/market-plan.md)

The 90-day plan rests on three parts: Korean developer channels, two marketing angles, and an audit of SerpApi's Korean coverage.

## Channels, in priority order
| Channel | What I'd do there | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| velog | Technical tutorials: SerpApi in Python and Next.js apps, handling the JSON response, Naver vs Google results | 2 posts a month |
| Inflearn and Nomad Coders communities | Answer students' data-collection and API questions; write short guides | Weekly |
| Naver Cafe and Kakao open chats (개발자 오픈채팅방) for AI, Python, growth hacking | Answer scraping and API questions, link docs or examples when they help | Weekly |
| OKKY, GeekNews, Disquiet | Share launches and tutorials; join discussions | When there's news |
| Kakao Brunch | Longer non-technical pieces for founders and marketers | Monthly |

X and Reddit stay secondary for the Korean market.

## Marketing angles
- Global expansion: pitch SerpApi to Korean companies that sell or watch markets abroad (e-commerce, fashion platforms) as a way to see search results in the US, Europe or Southeast Asia without running their own proxy networks. The gl, hl and location parameters make this concrete.
- AI startups: pitch SerpApi to Korea's AI startups as the infrastructure-free way to give their LLMs real-time search. My Korean AI search agent is the live example.

## Coverage audit
My feedback report is this audit: accuracy of Google Korea and Naver results, and gaps in Korean documentation.

## Ground rules
- In communities, say who I am; until hired, never present myself as speaking for SerpApi.
- Helpful answers first, links second; no copy-paste promotion.
- Company names are examples of fit only; don't imply they're customers or prospects.
- Know the basics of Korea's PIPA, but leave legal claims about scraping to SerpApi's legal team.
